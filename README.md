### What the translations contain

- **Volume I**, complete: the historical title matter, dedication and preface, the *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae*
  (Articles 1–366), the historical additions, Tables I–III, the contents, the manuscript appendix and the historical
  editor's closing note.
- **Volume II**, selection: the texts of the volume through its historical contents and imprint; the
  [translation guide](docs/english-german.md) lists them.
- **Volume III**, selection:
  - the 1799 dissertation on the fundamental theorem of algebra (Articles 1–24, ten footnotes, the closing statement
    of October 1797, four figures); the second proof (1815, Articles 1–20) and the third (1816, Articles 1–4); the
    proof of an algebraic theorem on positive and negative roots (1828); the contributions to the theory of
    algebraic equations (1849, Articles 1–20, with plate);
  - the reviews of 23 December 1815, 2 March 1816 (with Gauss's handwritten remarks) and 30 July 1849, and those of
    Coytier (6 March 1813) and Fourier (25 February 1833);
  - *General Investigations of the Infinite Series*, First Part (presented 30 January 1812, published 1813; Articles
    1–37, with the table of 102 rows), and the posthumous *Determination of Our Series by a Differential Equation of
    the Second Order* (Articles 38–57) with Schering's remarks;
  - *A New Method for Finding the Values of Integrals by Approximation* (presented 16 September 1814, published 1816;
    Articles 1–23, seven coefficient tables, seven numerical examples); the reviews of 10 February 1812 and
    26 September 1814; the review of Pfaff's *Methodus generalis* (1 July 1815);
  - *Remarks on logarithm tables* (1802–1851);
  - the posthumous *Theory of Interpolation Treated by a New Method* (Articles 1–41) with Schering's remarks;
  - the work on the attraction of a planetary orbit (1818, Articles 1–19) and its review of 9 February 1818;
  - the posthumous work on the arithmetic–geometric mean (Parts I and II as printed, the unfinished Article 11, the
    editorial continuation with Articles 12–26, and the table of means);
  - three works on lemniscatic functions; the *Collection of Calculations*; *Miscellaneous Topics, Chiefly on the
    Integral* [I.]; the papers on the theory of the new transcendental functions [II.]–[V.], among them *One Hundred
    Theorems* [IV.];
  - *Pentagramma mirificum*; Schering's final remarks; the historical contents and imprint.
- **Volume IV**, selection: the historical volume titles and *Theoria combinationis observationum erroribus minimis
  obnoxiae* (Theory of the Combination of Observations Subject to the Smallest Errors): First Part (presented
  15 February 1821, published 1823; Articles 1–22) and Second Part (presented 2 February 1823, published 1823;
  Articles 23–40).

The translations keep Gauss's formulations, hypotheses, qualifications, formulas and numerical values, including
printed irregularities and unfinished passages; the remarks of the historical editors (Schering, Dedekind and others)
are translated as theirs. Latin is translated into English and German; German originals are translated into English
and rendered in modern German in the German reader. The Latin and German originals are supplied separately with the
editable sources.

## Editable editions

Browse [original-language sources](original-language/) or [English and German sources](translations/). Each volume retains its build inputs and accompanying notices. The downloadable packages contain the sources corresponding to the linked readers. Text, mathematics, tables and included mathematical drawings are editable LaTeX; historical portraits are images. Modern fonts and reflow represent content rather than historical type identity.

## Authorship and sources

Carl Friedrich Gauss is the author of the historical mathematical text. Historical editors, correspondents and contributors retain their named credits. Original-language text is AI-transcribed, with AI-produced source comparisons and typesetting; the English and German texts are AI-translated. Exact model credits and source versions remain in the accompanying editions and guides. No human or independent review is claimed.

The translation guide credits H. Maser; James Caddall Morehead and Adam Miller Hiltebeitel; Paul Taylor and Bernard Leak; and the other named terminology references for their stated roles. See the [full scholarly credits](docs/english-german.md#translation-sources-and-terminology) and [citation metadata](translations/CITATIONS.bib). AI work does not replace historical or inherited human authorship.

These editions are freely available for study and teaching.

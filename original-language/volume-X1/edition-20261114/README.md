# Carl Friedrich Gauss — Werke X, Erste Abteilung

Original-language text from the 1917 volume *Nachträge zur reinen Mathematik*, published by the Königliche Gesellschaft der Wissenschaften zu Göttingen, in commission with B. G. Teubner, Leipzig.

This selection contains *Kleinere Veröffentlichungen*, including its title, the four publications on printed pages 3–8, Zimmermann's appended note and Schlesinger's editorial remarks. It also contains the *Arithmetik* title and *Einige asymptotische Gesetze der Zahlentheorie*, groups I–IV with the complete historical commentary on printed pages 16–18, followed by the letter to v. Zimmermann of 12 Merz 1797 and its historical editorial note on printed pages 19–21. The quadratic-residue section follows through groups I–IV on printed pages 22–36: the notes on sums, the 1805 letter to Olbers, the determination of the decident, the fifth proof of quadratic reciprocity, the new presentation of the third proof, and their historical commentary. Groups V–VI follow with the cubic and biquadratic residue notes and the notes, tables and lattice diagram on complex integers, with historical commentary on printed pages 37–50. Groups VII–X on printed pages 51–64 contain further biquadratic-residue notes, the general fundamental theorem, the notion of an intensor, and the theory of the residue 1+i, with diagrams, tables and Bachmann’s historical commentary. Groups XI–XII on printed pages 65–77 contain further residue formulas and the correspondence with Sophie Germain, Olbers and Bessel, together with Schlesinger’s historical remarks. Groups I–V of the notes on forms follow on printed pages 78–95: polygonal numbers, representations by three squares, ternary quadratic forms, class numbers and complex forms, with Bachmann’s and Schlesinger’s historical commentary. The Algebra section title and five letters concerning the fundamental theorem of algebra follow on printed pages 99–110, with Schlesinger’s historical commentary: two letters from Johann Friedrich Pfaff to Gauss and three from Gauss to Drobisch, Schumacher and Möbius. The circle-division notes, groups I–III, follow on printed pages 111–119: periods, the golden theorem and irreducibility, with Schlesinger’s historical commentary. The correspondence on the regular seventeen-sided polygon follows on printed pages 120–126: Pfaff to Gauss in 1802, with Pfleiderer’s construction, and Gauss to Gerling in 1819, with the complete historical footnotes. The notes on coefficients and power sums follow on printed pages 127–128, then the algebraic theorem and three letters to Schumacher on printed pages 129–134, with Schlesinger’s and Alfred Loewy’s historical commentary. The Analysis title, Klein’s attribution note and the complete *Exercitationes mathematicae*, with Schlesinger’s historical remarks, follow on printed pages 137–144. The early lemniscatic-function notes, subsection I on series expansions and addition theorems, follow with their complete historical remarks on printed pages 145–151. Subsection II follows on printed pages 152–159 with ten entries on double periodicity, product representations and series expansions of numerators and denominators, including the historical numerical calculations and editorial footnotes. Subsection III follows on printed pages 160–166 with seven entries on division of the lemniscate, numerical calculations, an editable diagram, and Schlesinger’s complete historical remarks on subsections II–III. Subsection IV follows on printed pages 167–171 with six entries of miscellaneous formulas and Schlesinger’s complete historical remarks. The notes on the arithmetic-geometric mean follow through subsections I–VIII on printed pages 172–216, including their series, numerical calculations and relations to the perimeter of an ellipse, differential and functional equations, the nine entries on functions arising from the arithmetic-geometric mean, an editable geometric diagram, and the fourteen entries of the general theory of the lemniscatic sine with both historical manuscript-variant footnotes, followed by the notes on differentiation and the five entries on algorithms for arithmetic-geometric means and the three entries on the bilinear algorithm with its historical manuscript-variant footnote. Subsection IX, entries [1]–[14], follows on printed pages 217–231 with the relations between the infinitely many arithmetic-geometric means, the numerical examples, series transformations, elliptic integrals and rectification of the ellipse. The correspondence on printed pages 232–251 contains all eight letters: Pfaff to Gauss in 1799 and 1800, with the complete appendix from H. C. Schumacher’s Gaussiana; Gauss to Bessel in 1805 on coefficient tables and continued fractions; the exchanges between Schumacher and Gauss in 1808 and 1816 concerning Pedrayes’s problem, higher transcendental functions and arithmetic-geometric means; and Gauss to Bessel in 1828, with the two appended letters between Pfaff and Gauss. The historical postscripts and footnotes are included. The editorial introduction and the complete four-section outline of the theory of the arithmetic-geometric mean follow on printed pages 251–257, including the algorithms, differential relations, series and integral representations and their historical footnotes. The complete historical explanation of subsection I follows on printed pages 257–261, including the analysis explicitly credited to J. Horn, the inverse-function discussion and the historical account of the manuscript chronology. The complete historical explanation of subsection II follows on printed pages 261–266, with the series, elliptic-integral relations, notation distinctions, historical corrections and manuscript discussion. The complete historical explanation of subsection III follows on printed pages 266–267, including the limiting argument, its nonconstant counterexample and the connection with the lemniscatic integral. The complete historical explanation of subsection IV follows on printed pages 268–274, including the Euler and Jacobi discussion, series and integral formulas, manuscript chronology and the complete historical notes. The complete historical explanation of subsection V follows on printed pages 274–278, with the lemniscatic-sine and theta-function formulas, the transformations, the historical discussion and all six notes. The complete historical explanation of subsections VI–VII follows on printed pages 278–279, with the derivative formula and the discussion of the modular function. The complete explanation of subsection VIII follows on printed pages 279–280, including its theta-function relations, historical discussion and two notes. The complete historical explanation of subsection IX follows on printed pages 280–283, including the modular-function discussion, series, ellipsoid formulas and all seven notes. The complete historical explanations of the correspondence follow on printed pages 283–286, with the power-series argument, the quotation from Gauss’s letter to Olbers, the biographical discussion, all seven notes and Schlesinger’s signature. The notes belonging to the theory of transcendental functions follow through subsection I, entries [1]–[16], on printed pages 287–307, including the series and product identities, the arithmetic-geometric mean and elliptic-integral relations, and all eighteen historical notes. Subsection II follows on printed pages 308–310 with the solution of the inversion problem for the elliptic integral of the first kind. Subsection III follows through its opening and entries [2]–[8] on printed pages 311–320, with the ellipse-to-circle notes, both editable diagrams, the coefficient series, the inverse series, the potential formulas and all seven historical footnotes. The historical introduction and complete explanations of subsections I–III follow on printed pages 320–325, including the theta transformations, reference table, manuscript chronology, elliptic integrals, all five historical footnotes and Schlesinger’s signature. The series notes on F(α, β, γ, x) begin with the complete four articles on continued fractions, including both sets of auxiliary series, the historical manuscript-variant footnote, the theorem and transformation. Their complete historical remarks follow on printed pages 330–331, with the comparison formulas, Lambert note, manuscript chronology and Schlesinger’s signature. Section II contains all five articles and Schlesinger’s historical remarks on printed pages 332–337, with the series relations, differential transformations, continued fractions, numerical values, logarithmic derivatives and complete logarithmic calculations. Section III includes its title, all ten articles and Schlesinger’s historical remarks on printed pages 338–354, giving relations through number (46), the coefficient table, parameter shifts, differential equations and transformations, products and series, continued fractions and arithmetic-geometric mean formulas, with the historical footnotes. Section IV includes its title, all six articles and Schlesinger’s historical remark on printed pages 355–359, giving two further series relations, the trigonometric expansion and its coefficient recurrence, a transformation with its sixth-power example, differential equations for a further series and arguments about incomplete sums, with the historical notes. The correspondence includes the seven letters: Gauss to Bessel of 21 October 1810, Gauss to Olbers of 17 October 1811, Gauss to Bessel of 21 November and 18 December 1811, Gauss to Laplace of 30 January 1812, Gauss to Bessel of 5 May 1812, and Gauss to Laplace of 5 November 1812, on the logarithmic integral, transcendental and factorial functions, definitions, complex integration, logarithms, continued fractions, probability, least squares and ellipsoidal attraction, with all historical notes, on printed pages 360–379. Schlesinger’s complete remarks follow on printed pages 379–381, with quotations from Legendre and Laplace and the historical account of the manuscripts and certified copies. The following notes on series begin with the title and articles 1–8 of the method for summing a divergent series, including the differential relations, coefficient expansions, numerical calculation, entries from Leiste and the Tagebuch, and historical manuscript notes, on printed pages 382–385. Schlesinger’s complete commentary on printed pages 385–389 discusses Euler’s divergent series, asymptotic expansions and Bessel functions, with its historical quotations, references and notes. Section II, on the basic concepts of series, comprises six articles and Schlesinger’s remarks on printed pages 390–395. Section III, Questions on the metaphysics of mathematics, follows with its five entries, lattice diagram and Schlesinger’s remark on printed pages 396–397. Section IV gives six series for discontinuous functions and Schlesinger’s remark on printed pages 398–399. Section V contains its introduction, both printed articles on convergence and imaginary quantities, and the appendix on printed pages 400–406. Section VI follows with all nine articles on complex quantities, their boundaries, integration and periodic expansions, including the boundary diagram, through printed page 419. Section VII includes all five articles on the convergence of the midpoint-equation expansion, with the numerical tables and historical editorial notes, through printed page 428. The following correspondence includes the letter to C. F. Hindenburg of 8 October 1799, the letters to Schumacher of 4 and 6 December 1849, 5 February and 1 September 1850, and the letter to H. G. Grassmann of 14 December 1844, with their historical notes, on printed pages 429–437. The historical remarks on sections V and VI and the complete mathematical explanation of section VII follow on printed pages 437–442. The complete historical commentary on the correspondence follows on printed pages 442–447, including the manuscript chronology, J. Horn’s quoted calculation, all historical notes and Schlesinger’s signature. The Geometry title and the four notes of Ansätze zur transzendenten Trigonometrie follow on printed pages 451–452, with all five diagrams. Stäckel’s complete historical commentary follows on printed pages 453–456, with its four diagrams, functional equations and spherical-trigonometry formulas. The note on spherical trigonometry and Stäckel’s historical remark follow on printed pages 457–458, retaining the attribution to Wittstein’s textbook. The Geometry correspondence includes letters [1]–[7], dated 19 March–21 April 1836, on printed pages 459–467, with nine diagrams and the historical footnotes. Stäckel’s historical closing remark follows on printed page 468. The notes on manifolds of n dimensions begin with section [I], Über die Ermittelung des kleinsten Werthes für das Maass des Zwanges, on printed pages 469–472, with the quotation from A. Ritter’s 1853 dissertation and its historical footnote. Section [II] follows with parts [1] and [2] on printed pages 473–481, from the 1850/51 lecture Über die Methode der kleinsten Quadrate as recorded by August Ritter, followed by Stäckel’s historical remark on printed pages 481–482. The diary section follows with its historical title and the complete first manuscript sheet, containing the seven entries dated 30 March–24 May 1796 and both continued fractions, transcribed as editable text and drawings. The title of the printed diary, the complete historical prefaces by Felix Klein and Ludwig Schlesinger, and entries 1–112 with their complete historical commentary follow on source leaves 512–581, ending before entry 113.

Carl Friedrich Gauss: Werke, Band VI (1874)
Original-language LaTeX transcription — source leaves 2–669.

This 919-page reader includes the historical titles and portrait; the works from De elementis ellipticis Palladis through Tafel für die Sonnen-Coordinaten and Neue Aussicht zur Erweiterung des Gebiets der Himmelskunde; the Refractionstafeln; the letters to Schumacher dated 1 April 1843 and 23 November 1847 as printed through source leaf 201; the division title Beobachtungen und Rechnungen; the Ceres reports of December 1801 and February, March, April, May, June, October and November 1802; the Pallas reports of June, July, August, October, November and December 1802; the letters to Bode of 10 September 1802 and 3 March 1803; the Mercury transit report of November 1802; and the complete Tafeln für die Störungen der Ceres, including the letter dated 26 December 1802, its four Anmerkungen, and all five tables for longitude, radius vector and latitude through source leaf 248; the Pallas reports of April/May, June and July 1803; the Ceres report of September/October 1803; Ceres und Pallas of March 1804; Neuer Comet of May 1804; and the two letters to Bode dated 30 September and 12 November 1804, including their complete observational tables and orbital elements; and Über einen neuen vom Inspector Harding in Lilienthal entdeckten höchst merkwürdigen Wandel-Stern, with its quoted letters, orbital elements, observation comparisons, stellar positions and twenty-row ephemeris; and Einige Nachrichten über den neuen Planeten, including its account of Harding’s discovery, the observations of Juno, its orbital dimensions and naming, and the historical description of its ephemeris. It also contains the Juno report of November 1804, with its comparison star, second orbital elements, discussion of the planetary orbits and seventeen observation comparisons; and Beobachtete Sternbedeckung, with both observations of 16 October 1804. The Juno report of December 1804 is also included, with its third orbital elements, thirty-one observation and comparison rows, four brightness values and complete discussion of the planet’s visibility. The Juno report of February 1805 and the Ceres report of March 1805 are included with their orbital elements, observations, comparison star, arguments and signatures. The Pallas report of April 1805 is included with its orbital elements, historical argument and signature von Zach. The Juno report of May 1805 contains the Palermo observations, their comparison with the fourth orbital elements, the fifth elements and the complete historical discussion. The two reports on the comets of 1805 contain the observations, orbital elements, quoted correspondence, coordinate formulas, distances and brightness values, with both signatures von Zach. The letter to Bode of 14 March 1806 contains the observations of Ceres, Pallas and Juno, the revised elements of the second comet of 1805, nineteen comparisons with observations, and the complete historical discussion of the comets and Gauss’s method for determining planetary orbits. The Pallas and Juno report of March 1806 contains the account of their renewed observation, the differences from the predicted positions, Gauss’s description of the observing instruments and difficulties, and the historical announcement of his work on planetary orbits. The letter to von Zach of 20 May 1806 includes the stellar identifications, parabolic and elliptical orbital elements of the second comet of 1805, both twenty-one-row comparisons with observations, the elements of the comet of 1772, and the complete discussion of orbital uncertainty and possible perturbations. The two letters to von Zach of 8 July 1806 contain the comparison of Bouvard’s observations with the parabolic and elliptical comet orbits, the discussion of parallax, aberration and methods for determining orbits, the ninth elements of Pallas with six epochs, the opposition predictions, the comparison of three observations of Juno and the historical description of the Pallas ephemeris. The letter to Bode of 30 July 1806 and the letters to von Zach of 25 August 1806 and 3 January 1807 contain the epochs and orbital elements of Pallas, Juno and Ceres, the discussion of their predicted appearances and brightness, the description of Gauss’s work on planetary orbits, and three historical notes. The letter to von Zach of 10 March 1807 contains the Pallas observations, comparison stars and discussion of the Ceres opposition. The letter to Bode of 8 May 1807 includes the two observations, discussion of orbital uncertainty and full fifty-row comparison with observations. The Vesta report of July 1807 contains the discussion of the new planet’s observation and orbital calculation, the complete comparison of eighty-four observations with the second elements, and the historical comments on observational corrections and meridian observations. The Pallas reports of 4 and 8 August 1812 contain the Göttingen, Bremen and Paris observations, comparisons with the orbital elements, both opposition results, the two sets of seven corrections, comparisons of eight oppositions and the predictions for the following year. The letter to Bode of 22 August 1812 and the Juno report of 9 September 1812 contain observations of Pallas and Juno, orbital corrections and elements, opposition results, and the discussion and table concerning the distances between the orbits of Ceres and Pallas. The Vesta report of 24 December 1812 contains the Göttingen, Seeberg and Marseille observations and comparisons, orbital elements and brightness values, and the concluding account of Nicolai’s work on the second comet of 1811. The Comet report of July 1813 contains the correspondence of 5 and 12 July, Olbers’s seven observations, Enke’s orbital elements and the direction of motion. The Comet report of 3 July 1815 contains the two Göttingen observations and fifteen Padua observations, the parabolic and elliptical orbital elements, a seven-row ephemeris, and the complete discussion of the orbit, brightness and period. The Comet report of 18 September 1815 contains twelve comet observations, the orbital elements calculated by Bessel and Nicolai, their comparison with the observations, three Juno observations and their comparison, the opposition position and new Juno elements, and the complete accompanying discussion. The letter of 18 July 1817 contains the full discussion of the instruments, polar-height differences and the centre of the Sun; the polar-star observations and their reductions; the example of observations away from culmination with its calculations and historical footnote; and the fifteen solar observations, their reductions, the probability and refraction discussion, and the historical handwritten annotations. The collected letters to Bode of 17 March and 15 August 1817 contain the discussion of the Pallas ephemeris, the eight observed phases of the lunar eclipse, the account of the new observatory and its instruments, the polar-star and solar observations with their means, and the complete discussion of the differing latitude determinations and astronomical journeys. The stellar-occultation report submitted on 8 March 1818 contains all eleven recorded times, including the paired observations by Gauss and Harding. The two Comet reports of March and April 1818 contain the orbital elements, ten-row ephemeris, comparison-star observations, historical handwritten star observations, and the complete discussion of the orbit, visibility and brightness. Borda’s Kreis contains the complete account of aligning the line of sight with the circle, the definitions of the three axes and their errors, the geometric and trigonometric formulas, the observational procedures for determining and correcting the errors, the historical footnote, and the methods for horizontal and vertical adjustment. Instrumente contains the description of the new observatory and Repsold’s meridian circle, the account of its construction and adjustment, nineteen polar-star determinations with their weights and probable errors, ten observations of Uranus and their comparison with the tables, the opposition results, and the twenty-three entries in Shelton’s clock register with the full concluding discussion. The letter to Bode dated 7 September 1818 contains the comparison of the Pallas opposition calculations, the account of the improved meridian circle, both formulas for observational weights, twenty polar-star determinations, ten Uranus observations, five Saturn observations, two Pallas observations, the discussion of the observing threads, and the Uranus comparisons and opposition results. The Comet report of 18 February 1819 contains the observations of both comets discovered in November 1818, the Mannheim, Göttingen, Seeberg and Königsberg tables, the parabolic and elliptical orbital elements, ten comparisons with observations, the historical note about Seeberg time, and the complete discussion of orbital uncertainty, visibility and possible identity with the first comet of 1805. The Comet report of 24 May 1819 contains the complete discussion of the comet’s identity, orbital period and perturbations, both sets of orbital elements, the twelve-row ephemeris, two additional positions, and the concluding appeal for further observations. Instrumente, dated 18 October 1819, contains the complete description of Reichenbach’s transit telescope and its adjustment, the account of stellar visibility and the lifting apparatus, eighteen Saturn observations, twelve Vesta observations, thirteen observations of 85 Pegasi with their mean, and the discussion of that star’s proper motion. Instrumente from the Astronomisches Jahrbuch for 1822 contains the account of the 1819 comet, nine observations, parabolic orbital elements, nine local and twelve external comparisons, the full account of the transit telescope and its adjustment, six Jupiter observations, the discussion of instrumental flexure and absolute zenith distances, ten polar-star observations with their weights and mean, the historical footnote, and the concluding description of the reversing apparatus. The letter to Bode of 21 March 1820 contains eighteen Saturn observations, twelve Vesta observations, six Pallas observations, six Mars observations, and the concluding remarks on Ceres, the reduction tables and the Reichenbach meridian circle. The letter to Bode of 3 September 1820 contains the discussion of the Pallas ephemeris, the meridian-circle observations and their reduction, thirty-two solar declinations, twenty stellar zenith distances with collimation errors and their mean, and the concluding comparison. The review dated 6 February 1813 discusses Bode’s Astronomisches Jahrbuch for 1814 and 1815, including all sixty-five articles and notices, astronomical observations, methods of determining orbits, parallax, instruments and atmospheric hypotheses. The review dated 8 March 1813 discusses Burckhardt’s lunar tables, their comparison with Bürg’s tables, the arrangement of the lunar equations, long-period inequalities and horizontal parallax.

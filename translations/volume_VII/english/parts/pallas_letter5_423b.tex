\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}I cannot help writing to you at least a few words to say that I have nearly finished the work I wrote to you about recently and that, unless I am entirely mistaken, it will turn out more satisfactorily than I could have hoped. Yesterday I first calculated from the first 4 oppositions the normal elements that, combined with the previously integrated perturbations, reproduced those oppositions exactly. To my great alarm, they differed from the 5th opposition by \(4'\) and from the 6th by \(20'\). This led me to discover an error that had crept into my entire calculation. I had taken the principal part of the perturbation of the mean motion with the wrong sign. Today I put everything right. With the normal elements computed preliminarily in a rough calculation using only the small tables, the differences will be
\begin{center}\begingroup\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrr}
in the 6 longitudes & \(8''\) & \(1''\) & \(8''\) & \(0''\) & \(6''\) & \(5''\)\\
in the 6 latitudes & \(2''\) & \(5''\) & \(6''\) & \(5''\) & \(17''\) & \(3''\)\\
\end{tabular}\endgroup\end{center}
Further refinement is still needed (the latitude of the 5th opposition was, as you know, poorly observed, and I thought it might be off by \(30''\)). The purely elliptic elements that agreed \textls[180]{best} differed

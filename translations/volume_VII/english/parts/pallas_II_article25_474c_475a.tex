\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}We shall now indicate a very simple method for calculating in full the perturbations of the order of the square of the perturbing force. Let \(Q\mathrm{d}t\) be the instantaneous variation of an element, for example of \(\AscendingNode+\delta\AscendingNode\). Then \(Q\) will depend on the elements of the perturbed planet and of the perturbing planet. If we substitute the values of these elements that are correct to the first order in the perturbations, it is clear that \(Q\) will be correct to the second order (inclusive). Let \SourceLeaf{475}\(a,\ b,\ c,\ e,\ f,\ g\) be the mean values of the elements of the perturbed planet and \(a+\Delta a,\ b+\Delta b,\ c+\Delta c,\ e+\Delta e,\ f+\Delta f,\ g+\Delta g\) the values correct to the first order. Let \(a',\ b',\ c',\ e',\ f',\ g',\ a'+\Delta a'\) etc. denote the same quantities for the perturbing planet. We may suppose
\[
\begin{alignedat}{2}
Q=Q^0&{}+\Delta a\cdot\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}Q^0}{\mathrm{d}a}\right)&&{}+\Delta a'\cdot\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}Q^0}{\mathrm{d}a'}\right)\\[3pt]
&{}+\Delta b\cdot\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}Q^0}{\mathrm{d}b}\right)&&{}+\Delta b'\cdot\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}Q^0}{\mathrm{d}b'}\right)\\[3pt]
&{}+\Delta c\cdot\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}Q^0}{\mathrm{d}c}\right)&&{}+\Delta c'\cdot\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}Q^0}{\mathrm{d}c'}\right)\\[3pt]
&\quad\text{etc.}&&\quad\text{etc.,}
\end{alignedat}
\]
\noindent if we restrict ourselves to quantities of the second order.

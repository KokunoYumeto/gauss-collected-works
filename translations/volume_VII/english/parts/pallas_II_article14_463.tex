\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}If, in place of the two forces \(mT\), \(mV\), we introduce two others \(mR\), \(mS\) that also act in the plane of the orbit, the first parallel to the line of apsides and the second perpendicular to it, or, more precisely, if these forces are parallel to the radii drawn from the centre of the sphere to the points \(P\), \(\Pi\), we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
mR&=\chi\cos PQ=-\xi\cos XP-\eta\cos YP-\zeta\cos ZP\\
mS&=\chi\cos\Pi Q=-\xi\cos X\Pi-\eta\cos Y\Pi-\zeta\cos Z\Pi,
\end{aligned}
\]
where we can substitute the values of the cosines given by equations 36—41. We also have
\[
\begin{aligned}
T&=R\cos(v-\tilde\omega)+S\sin(v-\tilde\omega)\\
V&=-R\sin(v-\tilde\omega)+S\cos(v-\tilde\omega).
\end{aligned}
\]

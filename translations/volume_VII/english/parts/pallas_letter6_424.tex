\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}My last two letters crossed yours. Since then I have finished my work on the perturbations of Pallas by Jupiter and obtained agreement beyond my boldest expectations. Here are the differences:

\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}In calculating the perturbations I used the elements that appear in the enclosed sheets\HistoricalNoteEight{Vol. VI, p. 63.]} on p. [1971] as No. IV. I have begun to repeat the entire calculation once more, dividing the period from 1803 to 1811 into seven or eight periods and using for each the particular elements given by my first calculation. But how astonished you will be, as I am, when I tell you that my ephemeris in the October issue of the M. C. is now more than \(1^{0}\) off, if I can rely on the results of the perturbation calculations! I believe I am sure of my ground, but the calculation is so lengthy that I shall have no real peace of mind until Pallas is found again. I am now impatient for favorable weather. If you are luckier in Bremen, you might look for it there too. According to the perturbed elements, the R.A. on 19 Dec. should be smaller by \(1^{0}10'\), on 10 Jan. by \(1^{0}40'\), and the southerly declination should be greater by \(12'\,.\,.\,.\,10'\) than in the ephemeris. At the {\AstroSymbols ☌} in 1809 with {\AstroSymbols ♃}, {\AstroSymbols ⚴} was perturbed very severely. If my calculations are confirmed to my satisfaction here too, I may treat Juno and Vesta similarly.
\par
\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}The paper whose notice I enclose will soon be printed.\quad.\quad.\quad.\quad.\quad.

Let $C,C',C''$ be the three heliocentric positions of the celestial body on the celestial sphere.
They lie respectively on the great circles $AB,A'B',A''B''$, between $A$ and $B$, $A'$ and $B'$,
$A''$ and $B''$ (Article 64, III). Moreover, the points $C,C',C''$ lie on the same great circle,
namely the one traced on the celestial sphere by the plane of the orbit. We denote by $r,r',r''$ the
three distances of the celestial body from the Sun, by $\rho,\rho',\rho''$ its distances from the Earth, and
by $R,R',R''$ the distances of the Earth from the Sun. Further, we set the arcs $C'C'',CC'',CC'$ respectively
$=2f,2f',2f''$, and
\[
r'r''\sin2f=n,\qquad rr''\sin2f'=n',\qquad rr'\sin2f''=n''.
\]
\hypertarget{native-187}{}We therefore have
\[
\begin{gathered}
f'=f+f''\\[.4ex]
AC+CB=\delta,\qquad A'C'+C'B'=\delta',\\[.4ex]
A''C''+C''B''=\delta'',
\end{gathered}
\]
and also
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sin\delta}{r}&=\frac{\sin AC}{\rho}=\frac{\sin CB}{R}\\[.7ex]
\frac{\sin\delta'}{r'}&=\frac{\sin A'C'}{\rho'}=\frac{\sin C'B'}{R'}\\[.7ex]
\frac{\sin\delta''}{r''}&=\frac{\sin A''C''}{\rho''}=\frac{\sin C''B''}{R''}\mathpunct{\cdot}
\end{aligned}
\]

It is clear from this that as soon as the positions of the points $C,C',C''$ are known, the
quantities $r,r',r'',\rho,\rho',\rho''$ can be determined. We shall now show how these positions can be obtained from
the quantities
\[
\frac{n''}{n}=P,\qquad 2\left(\frac{n+n''}{n'}-1\right)r'^3=Q
\]
which we have already said are the starting point of our method.

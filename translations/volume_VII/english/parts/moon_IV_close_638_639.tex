higher than the third, it is easy to see that reduction
III would add only terms of the sixth order. We may therefore simply
write \(M=M^{\prime}\). — Once all the reductions have been carried out,
we finally find that to obtain \(\beta\) we must replace, in \(\theta\), \(E,\lambda,M,a\), respectively, with

\par\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}\textsc{Mayer}'s tables have another peculiarity. They use,
in place of the true anomaly \(M^{\prime}\), a quantity intermediate, as it were,
between the true and mean anomalies, \(M^{\prime\prime}\). A correction
depending on \(a\) is applied to the position of the mean node.
It serves to remove the parts whose arguments are \(a+\lambda\) and \(2a+\lambda\).
This gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
M^{\prime}={}&M^{\prime\prime}-2e\sin M^{\prime\prime}
 +\tfrac54ee\sin2M^{\prime\prime}\\
&+\tfrac{11}{8}nn\sin2E^{\prime}-\tfrac14gg\sin2\lambda
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda^{\prime\prime}
 +\left(\tfrac94n\varepsilon-\tfrac{207}{96}nn\varepsilon
 -\tfrac94n^3\varepsilon\right)\sin a
 -\tfrac{27}{32}n\varepsilon\varepsilon\sin2a,
\]

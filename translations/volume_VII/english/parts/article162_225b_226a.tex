In the \emph{second} case, in which $B''$ coincides with $B$, $D'$ will also coincide with them or with the opposite point. Thus $AD'-\delta$ and $A''D'-\delta''$ will be either $=0$ or $=180^0$, so from the equations in Article 143 we derive
\SourceLeaf{226}\[
\frac{n'r'}{n}=\pm\frac{\sin\epsilon'}{\sin\epsilon}\cdot
\frac{R\sin\delta}{\sin(z+A'D-\delta')}
\]
\[
\frac{n'r'}{n''}=\pm\frac{\sin\epsilon'}{\sin\epsilon''}\cdot
\frac{R''\sin\delta''}{\sin(z+A'D''-\delta')}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
&R\sin\delta\sin\epsilon''\sin(z+A'D''-\delta')\notag\\
&\qquad=PR''\sin\delta''\sin\epsilon\sin(z+A'D-\delta').
\end{aligned}
\]
It is clear from this that $z$ can be determined independently of $Q$ by $P$ alone (unless we happen to have $A'D''=A'D$ or $=A'D\pm180^0$, in which case we would arrive at the third case). Once $z$ is found, $r'$ will also be known. The values of the quantities $\dfrac{n'r'}{n}\mathpunct{\raisebox{.45ex}{,}}$
$\dfrac{n'r'}{n''}$ then give $\dfrac{n}{n'}$ and $\dfrac{n''}{n'}\mathpunct{\raisebox{.45ex}{;}}$ from these we finally also obtain $Q=2\left(\dfrac{n}{n'}+\dfrac{n''}{n'}-1\right)r'^3$. Clearly $P$ and $Q$ cannot therefore be regarded as independent data. They will supply only a single datum or else be inconsistent. The positions of the points $C$, $C''$ will remain arbitrary in this case, provided they are taken on the same great circle as $C'$.

In the \emph{third} case, in which $A'$, $B$, $B'$, $B''$ lie on the same great circle, $D$ and $D''$ will coincide respectively with the points $B''$, $B$ or with the opposite points. Hence, by combining equations VII, VIII, IX of Article 143, we find
\[
P=\frac{R\sin\delta\sin\epsilon''}{R''\sin\delta''\sin\epsilon}
=\frac{R\sin(l'-l)}{R''\sin(l''-l')}\mathpunct{\cdot}
\]
In this case, then, the value of $P$ is already given by the data of the problem itself, and the positions of the points $C$, $C'$, $C''$ will remain indeterminate.

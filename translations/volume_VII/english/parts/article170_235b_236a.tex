In the first hypothesis we must use the uncorrected times $t,t',t'',t'''$, since the distances from the Earth cannot yet be calculated. As soon as approximate values of the quantities $x',x''$ are known, however, we can also determine those distances approximately. Since the formulas for $\rho$ and $\rho'''$ are somewhat more complicated here, it is advisable to put off calculating the time correction until the distances have been found with sufficient precision, to avoid having to \SourceLeaf{236}repeat the calculation. It is therefore convenient to base this operation on the values of $x',x''$ yielded by the penultimate hypothesis, so that only the last hypothesis starts from the corrected times and values of $P',P'',Q',Q''$. The formulas to be used for this purpose are as follows.
\[
\tag*{VII.}
\rho'=x'-R'\cos\delta'
\]
\[
\tag*{VIII.}
\rho''=x''-R''\cos\delta''
\]
\[
\tag*{IX.}
\begin{aligned}
\rho\cos\beta={}&-R\cos B\cos(a-l)\\
&+\frac{1+P'}{P'\left(1+\dfrac{Q'}{r'^3}\right)}\bigl(\rho'\cos\beta'\cos(a'-a)+R'\cos B'\cos(l'-a)\bigr)\\
&-\frac{1}{P'}\bigl(\rho''\cos\beta''\cos(a''-a)+R''\cos B''\cos(l''-a)\bigr)
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
\tag*{X.}
\begin{aligned}
\rho\sin\beta={}&-R\sin B\\
&+\frac{1+P'}{P'\left(1+\dfrac{Q'}{r'^3}\right)}\bigl(\rho'\sin\beta'+R'\sin B'\bigr)\\
&-\frac{1}{P'}\bigl(\rho''\sin\beta''+R''\sin B''\bigr)
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
\tag*{XI.}
\begin{aligned}
\rho'''\cos\beta'''={}&-R'''\cos B'''\cos(a'''-l''')\\
&+\frac{1+P''}{P''\left(1+\dfrac{Q''}{r''^3}\right)}\bigl(\rho''\cos\beta''\cos(a'''-a'')\\
&\qquad{}+R''\cos B''\cos(a'''-l'')\bigr)\\
&-\frac{1}{P''}\bigl(\rho'\cos\beta'\cos(a'''-a')+R'\cos B'\cos(a'''-l')\bigr)
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
\tag*{XII.}
\begin{aligned}
\rho'''\sin\beta'''={}&-R'''\sin B'''\\
&+\frac{1+P''}{P''\left(1+\dfrac{Q''}{r''^3}\right)}\bigl(\rho''\sin\beta''+R''\sin B''\bigr)\\
&-\frac{1}{P''}\bigl(\rho'\sin\beta'+R'\sin B'\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Formulas IX—XII follow readily from equations 1, 2, 3 of Article 112, provided the symbols used there are duly converted to those used here. The formulas plainly become much simpler if $B,B',B''$ vanish. Combining formulas IX and X gives both $\rho$ and $\beta$, and similarly combining XI and XII gives $\rho'''$ as well as $\beta'''$. Comparing these latitudes with the observed latitudes (which do not enter the calculation), if given, shows how precisely the latitudes of the first and last observations can be represented by the six elements fitted to the remaining data.

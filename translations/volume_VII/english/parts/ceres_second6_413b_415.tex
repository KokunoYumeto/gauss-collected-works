\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}. . . . .\quad Unfortunately, I have yet to finish my calculation of the perturbations of {\AstroSymbols ⚳}. I have not always worked on it steadily, and it has also cost me far more effort than I expected, in places perhaps more than was necessary. But one learns how best to use a method only by applying it. I had started with the perturbations in latitude. In my first calculation I had left out everything that depended on the eccentricities. \textsc{Oriani} had included just one term. I have now retained all the equations I found exceeding \(1''\), including a couple that depend on the product of the eccentricities and are thus of order 3 (since the inclination enters all the latitude equations in any case). In a sense I have already reaped the fruits of this work. You will recall that since 1803 I have complained that the latitudes at the {\AstroSymbols ☍} of 1803 could no longer be reconciled with those of 1801 using the motion of the \(\AscendingNode\) found from theory, and that I was forced to advance the \(\AscendingNode\) by \(3'\) in 1803. Similarly, at the {\AstroSymbols ☍} of 1804 the latitude did not agree with that of 1802. The inclination had to be reduced far more than the theory indicated.

With my latest elements, I had to assign the \(\AscendingNode\) a daily tropical motion of \(0''{,}241\) and the inclination a daily decrease of \(0''{,}0243\) to reconcile the observations, so that

\SourceLeaf{414}To my great delight, this is now unnecessary, and the observations agree quite well with the new latitude equations and secular motions. The remaining differences of a few arcseconds, small in themselves, can readily be explained by the fact that the radius vectors used still need correction, since the perturbation equations have yet to be applied in full.

If we choose to use the auxiliary quantity $u$ to find the time from the true anomaly, its value is most readily determined from equation IV. Formula II then gives $p$ from $r$, or $r$ from $p$, immediately and without a further calculation. Once $u$ is known, formula XI gives the quantity $\frac{\lambda k t}{b^{\frac{3}{2}}}$, which is analogous to the mean anomaly in the ellipse. We shall denote it by $N$, and from it we obtain the time elapsed since the passage through perihelion. Since the first part of $N$, namely $\frac{\lambda e(u u-1)}{2u}$, becomes $=\frac{\lambda r\sin v}{b\sin\psi}$ by formula VIII, computing this quantity in both ways can serve to check its accuracy. Alternatively, if desired, $N$ can be expressed without $u$ as follows.
\[
\mathrm{XII.}\quad N=\frac{\lambda\operatorname{tang}\psi\sin v}{2\cos\frac{1}{2}(v+\psi)\cos\frac{1}{2}(v-\psi)}-\log\frac{\cos\frac{1}{2}(v-\psi)}{\cos\frac{1}{2}(v+\psi)}\cdot
\]

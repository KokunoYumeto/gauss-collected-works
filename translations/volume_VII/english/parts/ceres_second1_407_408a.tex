{\large CORRESPONDENCE AND POSTHUMOUS PAPERS.\par}
\bigskip
[III. SECOND METHOD.]\par
[MAY—JULY 1805.]\par
\bigskip
[1.]\par
\medskip
\textsc{Gauss} to \textsc{Olbers}, Braunschweig, 10 May 1805.
\end{center}
\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}.\quad.\quad.\quad.\quad.\quad In the end I abandoned the method by which I had begun to calculate the perturbations of {\AstroSymbols ⚳}. The sheer amount of mechanical, lifeless calculation I saw ahead of me put me off. Even if Mr. \textsc{Bessel} and Mr. von \textsc{Lindenau}\quad.\quad.\quad.\quad.\quad had taken on all the calculations I could have entrusted to others, I would still have had more left to do than my patience could endure.

I have, however, already devised another method that can take me just as far and requires far less work, though of a more intricate kind. I have already made considerable headway in applying it to Ceres, although for now on a more restricted plan, since I go only as far as the 5th powers of the eccentricities of {\AstroSymbols ♃} and {\AstroSymbols ⚳}. The method appeals to me all the more because it allows me to put to good use a number of rather deep investigations, which I undertook some time ago, into special kinds of transcendental functions. I shall try to give you some idea of it in [the] future. I also hope to arrange things so that outside help will considerably lighten my work. In my present calculation for {\AstroSymbols ⚳}, it is too late for this (for I have already made too much \SourceLeaf{408}progress in the very part where I could use help). It will be possible when I repeat the calculation, as I shall need to do, since the extended perturbation equations will undoubtedly entail considerable changes in the elements themselves. I could also use such help if I eventually undertake this work for {\AstroSymbols ⚴} and {\AstroSymbols ⚵}, where it will be considerably more extensive.\quad.\quad.\quad.\quad.\quad.

Zodiac[u]s.
\end{center}

[To obtain an example of the case in which two limits divide the celestial sphere into several regions, with some regions containing no geocentric positions at all, others containing points that can be geocentric positions in two ways, and still others containing points that can be geocentric positions in four ways, choose the following for the]
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{Outer orbit}\quad &k=1&\qquad e&=0\\
\text{Inner orbit}\quad &k^{\prime}=0{,}8&g^{\prime}&=270^{0}
\end{aligned}
\qquad [i=90^{0}]
\]
[where the notation is the same as in the paper »Über die Grenzen der geocentrischen Örter etc.« Vol. VI p. 106. We then have the]
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{Formulas:}\qquad &\cos t^{\prime}=\frac{k^{\prime}}{k}\cos t\\[5pt]
&r^{\prime}=\frac{k^{\prime}}{1-e^{\prime}\sin t^{\prime}}\\[5pt]
&r^{\prime}\cos t^{\prime}-k\cos t=\Delta\cos\alpha\cos\beta\\[5pt]
&-k\sin t=\Delta\sin\alpha\cos\beta\\[5pt]
&r^{\prime}\sin t^{\prime}=\Delta\sin\beta.
\end{aligned}
\]

[In this case there are two separate limit lines that can cross themselves, and the] critical point [occurs for]
\[
\sin t^{\prime}=\BareRoot[3]{\frac{1-\dfrac{k^{\prime}k^{\prime}}{kk}}{e^{\prime}}}\mathpunct{\cdot}
\]

\textsc{Gauss} to \textsc{Olbers}, Göttingen, 24 July 1816.
\end{center}
\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}. . . . .\quad This year's opposition of Pallas [XI.] still agrees very well with my theory and confirms the need to increase Jupiter's mass. I now find \(\frac{\text{\AstroSymbols ☉}}{\text{\AstroSymbols ♃}}=1050\) and the probable uncertainty in this number very nearly \(=1\); \textsc{Laplace} finds 1070 from Saturn's motion, with a somewhat greater uncertainty; this difference is thus enormous; but I do not believe one is entitled to infer a different chemical affinity from it, since \textsc{Laplace}'s result rests on \textls[180]{his} theory of Saturn, developed by a method whose adequacy may be doubted. Incidentally, I do not yet wish \textls[180]{my} determination of Jupiter's mass to be made known. Each additional opposition of Pallas will increase the accuracy. An enormous amount of work, to be sure, since the calculation for a single opposition cannot be done in 14 days. I am very curious to see what results Juno will yield soon.\quad . . . . .
